Technical Methodology

Sounding & ROB Verification

The reliability of an onboard fuel quantity depends on the quality of the physical measurement, the applicable tank calibration data and the consistency of the calculation basis.

01. Tank Measurement

Fuel level is physically determined through an appropriate sounding or ullage measurement process.

  • Tank identification
  • Sounding / ullage reading
  • Temperature observation
  • Measurement condition
  • Relevant vessel records

02. Tank Calibration

The measured level is evaluated against the applicable vessel tank calibration information.

  • Tank calibration table
  • Sounding or ullage basis
  • Trim / list considerations where applicable
  • Reference condition

03. Quantity Determination

The determined volume is combined with the applicable product and measurement data to establish quantity.

  • Observed volume
  • Temperature
  • Density
  • Mass calculation

04. ROB Reconciliation

The calculated quantity is compared against vessel records and the fuel balance for the relevant period.

  • Opening ROB
  • Bunker received
  • Fuel consumption
  • Expected closing ROB
  • Actual closing ROB

ROB Quantity Control

A Transparent Fuel Balance

Reconciliation provides a structured way to compare the expected fuel balance with the measured closing ROB.

Opening ROB + Bunker Received − Consumption ± Adjustments = Expected Closing ROB
Variance = Actual Closing ROB − Expected Closing ROB

The calculation basis must remain consistent with the measurement method, vessel records and applicable contractual or operational requirements.

Important: A variance is an observation requiring investigation. It should not automatically be interpreted as a measurement error, shortage or loss.
